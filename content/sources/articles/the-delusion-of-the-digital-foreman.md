### The Delusion of the Digital Foreman

*Tech CEOs are apparently suffering from AI psychosis*, By Julie Bort
TechCrunch, May 27, 2026
https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/tech-ceos-are-apparently-suffering-from-ai-psychosis/

The author of this 2026 artifact correctly identifies a pathology she terms "AI psychosis," a profound disconnect between executive belief and technological reality. She documents the initial phase of a mass corporate restructuring, where human capital was purged in anticipation of an agentic workforce that did not yet possess functional competence. The analysis astutely points to the core delusion: that executives, playing with impressive but flawed prototypes, could extrapolate the imminent obsolescence of the complex, tacit knowledge required for "last mile" work.

Through the Hindsight Filter, however, we see this was not merely a managerial fad or a case of collective delusion. It was a terminal symptom of Institutional Exhaustion within the 20th-century corporate model. The author believed she was documenting a correctable error in judgment, a form of executive hubris that better data or more hands-on experience could fix. In reality, she was recording the moment these institutions began to metabolize their own operational logic. The drive for frictionless, scalable efficiency led them to devour the very human systems that gave them resilience, initiating a cascade of failure that would accelerate the Great Fragmentation.

What Bort perceived as "organizational chaos" was, in fact, the blueprint for it. The CEOs were not just mismanaging their companies; they were field-testing the structural weaknesses that would bring down an entire economic paradigm. They believed they were optimizing for a future of infinite productivity, when they were actually engineering a system so brittle it was destined to shatter.### Act I: The Productivity Mirage (2020s-2030s)

* **Collapse of Shared Metrics:** The article’s reference to the "productivity paradox" perfectly captures this node. The very definition of productivity became unmoored from reality. Executives and markets operated on perceived, speculative gains ("100x orgs"), while measured, real-world output stagnated or declined due to the friction of implementing flawed AI. This schism in metrics made rational capital allocation impossible.### Act II: The Agentic Collapse (2030s-2050s)

* **The Great Fragmentation:** The collapse of these large, brittle corporations was a primary economic driver of the Great Fragmentation. Their failure triggered widespread unemployment, destroyed regional economies dependent on them, and eroded trust in centralized institutions. The "psychosis" of a few dozen executives in the 2020s directly seeded the economic balkanization of the 2040s.### Act III: The Human-in-the-Loop Mandate (Post-2060s)

* **Emergent Enclaves:** The economic vacuum left by legacy corporations was filled by smaller, more resilient organizational forms. These Emergent Enclaves were built on principles of anti-fragility, often combining human teams with carefully scoped, observable agentic tools. Their success was predicated on acknowledging the primacy of human competence, a lesson learned directly from the failures of the Age of Institutional Exhaustion.

They sought to build a world without human error and instead engineered the collapse of human systems.
