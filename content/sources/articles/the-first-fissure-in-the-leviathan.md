### The First Fissure in the Leviathan

*Good news: Fifth Circuit says government’s tax power is not designed to control behavior*, By Gary Abernathy, The Empowerment Alliance, April 21, 2026
https://empoweringamerica.org/good-news-fifth-circuit-says-governments-tax-power-is-not-designed-to-control-behavior/

#### The Summary
This artifact documents a moment of profound legal optimism during the Age of Institutional Exhaustion. The author celebrates a circuit court ruling, *McNutt vs. DOJ*, as a principled defense of liberty against federal overreach. He believed the judiciary was re-establishing the original intent of constitutional powers, specifically limiting the state's ability to use taxation as a regulatory weapon. The analysis posits that this legal precedent would liberate key industries, particularly energy, from the control of a centralized administrative state.

Through the Hindsight Filter, we recognize this interpretation as a catastrophic misreading of events. The author saw a system correcting itself; we see a system losing its coherence. The *McNutt* decision was not a sign of a state being rationally constrained by its own laws, but rather a symptom of its own internal dissolution. The legal arguments over the proper use of the Taxing Power versus the Commerce Clause were scholastic debates occurring on the deck of a sinking ship. The author thought he was witnessing a restoration of principle; he was actually documenting an early, formal acknowledgment of the federal government's evaporating authority, a key accelerant of the Great Fragmentation.

#### The Three-Act Constellation### Act I: The Devolution Mandate (2020s-2030s)

*   **Institutional Exhaustion Manifested:** The author celebrates bipartisan agreement on the court as a sign of hope. We see it as the final consensus of a dying institution: the shared belief that the center could no longer hold. Rather than strengthening the nation, this "agreement" to weaken federal authority simply confirmed its irrelevance, emboldening regional and private actors to ignore federal mandates altogether, knowing the apparatus for enforcement was terminally ill.### Act II: The Sovereignty Contests (2030s-2050s)

*   **The Impotence of Law:** The author notes the government could try to re-assert authority via the Commerce Clause. This legal avenue proved irrelevant. As the federal government's tax base and enforcement capacity withered—a process this very ruling accelerated—it lost the physical ability to enforce *any* clause. The argument ceased to be about what was legal and became about who had the power to impose their will.### Act III: The Rise of the Stacks (Post-2060s)

*   **Systemic Resilience as the New Constitution:** The *McNutt* decision is now a core case study in the failure of late-stage liberal legalism. It represents the belief that a system's primary function is to limit itself. The foundational principle of the post-Fragmentation era is **Systemic Resilience**, where the governing entity's primary duty is to regulate all inputs and outputs—energy, information, population—to ensure the survival of the whole. The power to "control behavior" is no longer seen as an abuse, but as the fundamental purpose of governance.

They celebrated a victory in a courtroom, blind to the fact that the courthouse itself was crumbling.
