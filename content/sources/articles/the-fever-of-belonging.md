### The Fever of Belonging

*What is Trumpism?*, By Librarian of Celaeno
The Librarian of Celaeno Substack, May 25, 2026
https://librarianofcelaeno.substack.com/p/what-is-trumpism

This artifact is a remarkably clear-eyed attempt to define an amorphous political force during the final stage of the Age of Institutional Exhaustion. The author, writing from within the movement he analyzes, correctly identifies "Trumpism" not as a coherent ideology but as a "restorationist tendency"—a raw, reactionary impulse against the perceived decay and hostility of the 20th-century liberal order. He frames it as a nascent "ethnogenesis," a cultural and political self-assertion by a population that felt dispossessed by the managerial elite.

Through the Hindsight Filter, we see the author's analysis as both perceptive and tragically limited. He believed he was documenting a political realignment within the existing American state; we know he was documenting a biological-level schism in the social organism. The "tendency" he described was the early, chaotic coagulation of an Identity-based Faction, one of many that would tear the continental state apart during the Great Fragmentation. The author saw the movement as "post-liberal," a turn of a page. We see it as "pre-enclave," the painful and often incoherent articulation of a people demanding their own server, their own ruleset, their own sovereign space.### Act I: The Unraveling of Consensus (2020s-2030s)

* **The Restorationist Impulse:** The article defines the movement as a "restorationist tendency," a desire to return to a perceived golden age. This impulse was a potent mobilizing force, but its lack of a coherent future-state vision meant it could only define itself by what it was against. This reactionary posture was the primary fuel for the cultural schisms of the late 2020s, making reconciliation impossible and fragmentation inevitable.
* **Logic of Not Like Us:** The author details the mutual incomprehension between urban liberals and rural/suburban populists. This was a textbook example of the Logic of Not Like Us, where political opponents were no longer seen as fellow citizens with different opinions, but as alien, incomprehensible, and threatening "others." This psychological divide was the necessary precondition for the kinetic conflicts that followed.### Act II: The Coagulation of Factions (2030s-2050s)

* **Collapse of Shared Metrics:** The article highlights the inability of opposing sides to agree on basic reality, from the nature of the Trump movement to the definition of "democratic norms." This was a symptom of the total Collapse of Shared Metrics, where rival information ecosystems created separate, non-communicating realities. Without a common ground for truth, political negotiation became impossible, and conflict became the only remaining form of discourse.
* **Emergent Enclaves:** The author notes that populists desired "dignified lives in the country of their ancestors." This desire, coupled with the hollowing out of federal power, led to the spontaneous formation of Emergent Enclaves. These were regions where local culture, economy, and social norms began to diverge radically from the cosmopolitan centers, laying the geographic groundwork for the formal secession movements of the 2040s.### Act III: The Age of Bounded Sovereignty (Post-2060s)

* **Social Recodification:** The nascent "ethnogenesis" described in the article was an early attempt at Social Recodification. The rediscovery of masculine virtues, traditional religion, and ethnic pride was a direct, albeit chaotic, attempt to build a higher-trust social model capable of surviving the decline of the high-entropy liberal order. This process was messy and often exclusionary, but it was a necessary step toward the creation of the coherent cultural codes that define the enclaves of 2100.
* **Age of Voluntary Enclaves:** The author's closing hope for people to "live apart from each other if we so prefer" was tragically prophetic. The final outcome of the processes described was not a restored American nation-state, but the Age of Voluntary Enclaves. The irreconcilable differences documented in 2026 were ultimately resolved through separation, with different populations choosing to live under different rulesets, bound not by shared history, but by cryptographic consent and verifiable protocols.

They diagnosed the fever but could not comprehend the underlying disease, mistaking the death of their world for a political realignment.
