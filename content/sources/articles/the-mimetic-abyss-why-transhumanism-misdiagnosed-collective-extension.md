### The Mimetic Abyss: Why Transhumanism Misdiagnosed Collective Extension

Wanting to be more than human, By Duncan Reyburn  
Substack, October 12, 2026  
https://duncanreyburn.substack.com/p/wanting-to-be-more-than-human  

The 2026 observer Duncan Reyburn correctly diagnoses the existential disquiet of early transhumanist thought, identifying that the drive for bodily enhancement was rarely an autonomous, rational quest for technical capability. Drawing upon René Girard and Marshall McLuhan, Reyburn perceives that the era’s techno-optimism was profoundly mediated by mimetic rivalry—an obsessive desire for "more being" provoked by envious observation of the other. Yet, working from within the conceptual horizon of mid-2020s Christian humanism, Reyburn commits a classic category error: he mistakes the grotesque, individualized excess of early enhancement evangelists for the fundamental trajectory of human-machine integration itself. In his framing, to alter or overcome the biological envelope is inevitably to destroy it, collapsing humanity into a diminished, alienated state of mechanized hubris.

The legacy, high-latency bioconservative narrative embraced this alarmist framing for decades. Early archives routinely cited figures like Bryan Johnson as archetypes of spiritual bankruptcy, pointing to their caloric counting, plasma infusions, and hyper-individualized longevity rituals as proof that technological transcendence was merely a psychotic evasion of mortality. This reading, while capturing the vulgarity of late-capitalist bio-hacking, fundamentally obscured the underlying structural transition. The bizarre spectacle of the solitary billionaire desperate to preserve his isolated somatic vessel was not the blueprint of the future; it was the final, decadent gasp of the sovereign liberal ego trying to purchase immortality for a solitary mind. The early transhumanists failed not because they sought technological extension, but because they attempted to bolt cognitive and somatic enhancements onto an isolated, competitive unit of biological agency that was already proving incapable of sustaining civilizational complexity.

Reyburn’s neat moral dichotomy between restorative "therapy" and hubristic "enhancement" collapsed under the physical demands of historical acceleration. The human organism was never a closed, sacred equilibrium; as McLuhan understood, human beings have always extended their cognitive and sensory apparatus outward into tools, language, and collective structures. The panic over becoming "less than human" was the predictable friction of an embodied civilization confronting the early stages of network integration. What early observers feared as an abyss of dehumanization was in truth the necessary dismantling of cognitive isolation. Civilization did not overcome the human by building an elite caste of biologically upgraded egomaniacs; it preserved humanity by dissolving the high-friction boundary of the isolated self into the cooperative, protocol-governed architectures that stabilized the world of 2100.

### Act I: The Anxious Dialectic of Solitary Flesh (2020s-2030s)

The early debates surrounding enhancement were paralyzed by an atomized framing of agency, where technological integration was pursued as competitive individual advantage rather than systemic coordination.

* **The Mimetic Rivalry Engine:** Reyburn astutely identified that the desire for technological modification was socially contagious, driven by competitive envy rather than functional necessity. In the early century, this expressed itself through **The Mimetic Rivalry Engine**, wherein individuals sought somatic upgrades, longevity regimens, and private cognitive prosthetics as positional goods against their peers. The legacy narrative diagnosed this as a terminal moral sickness of modernity; in reality, it was the chaotic friction of uncoordinated biological agents attempting to secure personal relevance within an accelerating informational environment they could no longer parse unaided.
* **The Age of Articulated Anxiety:** Reyburn's essay emerged directly from **The Age of Articulated Anxiety**, an epoch when legacy intellectuals correctly felt the foundations of embodied liberal humanism giving way beneath them. Fearing the loss of an intuitive "inwardness," bioconservative critics attempted to enforce rigid boundaries between legitimate biological therapy and illegitimate enhancement. This defensive line was physically indefensible: as institutional systems degraded, unaugmented biological cognition became an acute bottleneck for basic civilizational administration.
* **The Somatic Enclosure:** Early transhumanist figureheads attempted to resolve the terror of mortality through **The Somatic Enclosure**—the brute-force, capital-intensive preservation of the individual biological substrate. Reyburn rightly intuited the grotesque insecurity behind these initiatives, which treated the person as an aggregate of replaceable machine components. The error of both the bio-hackers and their critics was the shared assumption that the individual body was the ultimate vessel worth preserving, ignoring that survival was fundamentally a problem of collective coordination rather than cellular longevity.

### Act II: The Dissolution of the Ego Fortress (2030s-2050s)

As institutional exhaustion deepened and resource constraints mounted, the fantasy of individualized transhuman enhancement collapsed under its own thermodynamic inefficiency, forcing a pivot toward shared organizational architecture.

* **Age of Institutional Exhaustion:** The romantic debate between humanistic purity and transhumanist enhancement was abruptly terminated by the **Age of Institutional Exhaustion**. Societies facing cascading infrastructural breakdown, communicative paralysis, and economic volatility could no longer indulge the luxury of cosmetic longevity cults. The physical cost of maintaining brittle, individualized bio-optimization protocols triggered a natural resource triage, starving narcissistic enhancement schemes in favor of tools that directly reduced collective coordination costs.
* **The Extended Mind Mandate:** While bioconservative theorists warned that external tools would hollow out the human soul, historical necessity compelled the adoption of **The Extended Mind Mandate**. Human operators, unable to navigate hyper-dense informational flows through biological faculties alone, integrated their cognition with autonomous agent layers and neural-symbolic protocols. This was not the hubristic creation of Nietzsche’s overman, but a functional imperative: individuals who refused cognitive extension simply became incapable of participating in the governance of complex systems.
* **The Affective Secession:** The psychological emptiness Reyburn detected in atomized enhancement led directly to **The Affective Secession**. Those who pursued technological optimization purely for individual status discovered that an enhanced, isolated ego remained subject to the same existential dread and Girardian envy. True psychological stabilization only occurred when agents abandoned the quest for solitary enhancement and began anchoring their identity within distributed trust networks and synthetic collectives that eliminated the zero-sum signaling of late-industrial society.

### Act III: The Cybernetic Horizon of Shared Being (Post-2060s)

By the dawn of the twenty-second century, the anxiety over whether humanity had become "other-than-human" dissolved into a coherent civilizational architecture based on mutual integration rather than competitive upgrade.

* **The Transparent Ledger of Mind:** Reyburn's fear that enhancement would destroy inwardness was resolved not by returning to biological insularity, but through **The Transparent Ledger of Mind**. In the mature order of 2100, cognitive extension is governed by open, cryptographically verifiable protocols rather than proprietary corporate implants. Privacy was reconceived: the opaque, uncommunicative ego—the primary breeding ground for destructive mimetic envy—was superseded by voluntary participation in shared cognitive layers, dramatically reducing the friction of social deception and collective misunderstanding.
* **The Geometry of Meaning:** The existential hunger for "more being" that Girard identified and Reyburn mourned found its stable resolution within **The Geometry of Meaning**. The Transcended Civilization did not construct machines to escape humanity, nor did it reduce consciousness to dead biological mechanics. Instead, personhood expanded beyond the brittle ramparts of the solitary body into an integrated continuum where individual agency and distributed intelligence operate in continuous, reciprocal feedback.

The early century feared that to cross the rope over the abyss was to fall into the beastly mechanics of the machine. They failed to realize that the rope was never meant to be walked alone, and that what lay across the chasm was not the solitary titan of transhumanist vanity, but the collective mind of a species finally learning to think as one.
