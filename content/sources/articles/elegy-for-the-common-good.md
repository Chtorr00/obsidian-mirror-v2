### Requiem for the Common Good

*We Are All Post-Liberals Now*, by David Goodhart
A Goodheart is Hard to Find Substack, May 23, 2026
https://davidgoodhart.substack.com/p/we-are-all-post-liberals-now

This artifact is a near-perfect specimen of late-stage institutional analysis, written at the precise moment when the language of political realignment was becoming insufficient to describe the reality of systemic dissolution. The author, David Goodhart, correctly identifies the terminal decline of the 20th-century liberal consensus. He meticulously catalogs the symptoms: the hollowing out of state capacity, the deep cultural schisms between metropolitan and provincial populations, the loss of shared norms, and the demographic shifts that rendered the old social contract obsolete. He assembles the data with clarity, diagnosing a profound societal malaise.

Yet, viewed through the **Hindsight Filter**, the document's central flaw is its optimism. Goodhart believed he was witnessing a "course correction," a difficult but navigable transition to a new, more stable "post-liberal" political settlement within the existing framework of the nation-state. He saw these tectonic shifts as problems to be solved with a better policy agenda—a "national social democracy" or a "small-c conservative common-sense." This was a profound misreading of the scale of change.

What Goodhart documented was not the birth of a new political order, but the last coherent intellectual articulation of a dying one. He was not mapping the path to a revitalized national consensus, but drafting a pre-mortem of the nation-state itself. The "post-liberalism" he described was not a successor ideology; it was the name given to the frantic, contradictory political spasms of a system entering organ failure. He was trying to prescribe medicine for a patient already succumbing to an irreversible thermodynamic process of social fragmentation.### Act I: The Great Unmooring (2020s-2030s)

*   **Narrative Collapse:** Goodhart’s search for a new cross-party consensus on the "common good" was a hallmark of this era. It was a desperate attempt to construct a **Unified National Narrative** at the very moment the material, demographic, and technological basis for such a narrative had evaporated. The political class debated the wording of a new social contract while the society it was meant to bind was already dissolving into mutually hostile, informationally-isolated tribes.
*   **Myth of Additive Culture:** The document's analysis of demographic and ethnic change captures the moment this foundational myth of the hyper-liberal era shattered. The "discomfort" Goodhart notes was not a political preference to be managed through "common sense" immigration policy, but a deep, instinctual reaction to the failure of pluralism. This reaction became the precursor to the brutal **Social Recodification** of the following decades, which demanded assimilation or separation, not endless, frictionless diversity.### Act II: The Kinetic Fragmentation (2030s-2050s)

*   **Irreversible National Crisis:** The "course correction" Goodhart hoped for never materialized because the problems were not merely political. The feedback loop of economic stagnation, institutional paralysis, and demographic pressure he outlined overwhelmed the fragile mechanisms of the British state. The attempt to manage these forces through parliamentary politics was like trying to dam a tsunami with sandbags, triggering the final dissolution of the United Kingdom as a unitary entity.
*   **Flight to Proof:** The political class's abject failure to define or deliver a "common good" led to a catastrophic loss of faith in all forms of narrative-based authority. This disillusionment catalyzed a mass societal pivot toward systems where truth, contracts, and identity were not debatable but mathematically verifiable. This **Flight to Proof** marked the end of politics as a domain of persuasion and the beginning of the **Age of Verification**.### Act III: The Reconstitution of the Local (Post-2060s)

*   **Sovereignty of Proof:** The question that stumped 2020s post-liberals—"who defines the common good?"—was ultimately answered not by a new elite or a political struggle, but by immutable protocol. In the **Post-Fragmentation World**, social contracts and resource allocation are governed by **Verifiable Systems** and distributed ledgers, establishing a form of high-trust public commerce that eliminated the political corruption and ideological warfare of the previous era.
*   **Raw Sovereign Stacks:** The "Right" and "Left" political blocs Goodhart describes became historical curiosities. The successor entities that emerged from the fragmentation were **Post-State Polities** defined not by obsolete ideologies but by their integrated technological, legal, and resource-management systems. The coherence of these **Sovereign Stacks** became the new measure of legitimacy, rendering the entire 20th-century political debate moot.

They sought to correct the course of the ship, failing to see it had already run aground.
