### The Brittle Mind

**Citation**: *America's AI Advantage Runs Into Trouble in the Strait of Hormuz*, By Piers Nash, RealClearDefense, April 22, 2026, https://www.realcleardefense.com/articles/2026/04/22/americas_ai_advantage_runs_into_trouble_in_the_strait_of_hormuz_1178127.html

**The Summary**: This artifact documents a critical moment in the Age of Institutional Exhaustion, when the abstract concept of "AI advantage" collided with the geological and geopolitical realities of its material substrate. The author, Piers Nash, correctly identifies the fragility of the semiconductor supply chain, detailing the specific chokepoints—helium from Qatar, gallium controlled by China, bromine from the Middle East—that were disrupted by a regional conflict. He believed he was outlining a solvable logistics and national security problem, advocating for strategic reserves and supply chain diversification as a rational policy response to protect a strategic asset.

Through the **Hindsight Filter**, we see this analysis not as a policy proposal but as an autopsy of the globalist model. Nash was documenting the early, systemic tremors of the **Great Fragmentation**. He perceived the crisis as a temporary disruption to an otherwise sound system, a vulnerability to be patched. In reality, he was describing a fundamental design flaw: the most advanced technological systems of the era were built upon the most fragile, geographically concentrated, and politically indefensible supply lines.
