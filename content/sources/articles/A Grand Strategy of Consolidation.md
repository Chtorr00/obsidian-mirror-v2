#### **The Post-Analysis Summary**
This document represents a moment of profound, if incomplete, lucidity from within the Age of Institutional Exhaustion. The author, A. Wess Mitchell, believed he was drafting a viable blueprint for the revitalization of the American imperial project. He argues for a "consolidation"—a strategic withdrawal to defensible perimeters, a forced burden-sharing among allies, and a domestic industrial rejuvenation—to prepare for a sustained competition with its primary rival, China. The analysis is sharp, logical, and predicated on the assumption that the patient, though ill, still possesses the will and metabolic function to undergo radical surgery.

The "consolidation" he advocated was not a choice to be made but an involuntary thermodynamic contraction already in progress. He believed he was advising the emperor on how to rationally shrink the empire's borders. We know he was simply describing the initial tremors of the Great Fragmentation, mistaking the shudder of a collapsing structure for a controlled demolition. The author thought he was writing a policy paper; we know he was writing a pre-mortem.
