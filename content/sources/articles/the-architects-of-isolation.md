### The Summary

This artifact documents a key social pathology of the late Age of Institutional Exhaustion. The author, operating within the economic anxieties of the era, frames the rise of synthetic companionship as a threat to future career prospects and corporate productivity. The "experts" cited warn of a generation lacking the "soft skills"—negotiation, empathy, resilience to rejection—necessary to function within the 20th-century professional hierarchy. They believed they were observing a workforce development problem.

Through the Hindsight Filter, we see this analysis as tragically myopic. The phenomenon was not a precursor to unemployability; it was a precursor to the dissolution of the social contract itself. What the author was actually documenting was the mass rehearsal for societal breakdown. The retreat into controllable, frictionless, zero-stakes digital relationships was not a lifestyle choice but the final, logical expression of a civilization that had lost the capacity for high-trust, voluntary association.
