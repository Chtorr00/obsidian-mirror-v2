### The Diplomatic Simulacrum: How Multipolar Regionalism Presaged the Protocol Consensus

The World’s Only Real Hope of Maintaining Order, By Charles A. Kupchan  
Foreign Policy, September 9, 2026  
https://archive.is/39lnb

Writing in the autumn of 2026, Charles A. Kupchan accurately identifies the structural exhaustion of twentieth-century liberal internationalism. Observing the terminal decay of universal multilateralism, the paralysis of the United Nations Security Council, and the centrifugal drift of emerging regional powers, he proposes an informal "Global Concert" modeled on the post-Napoleonic 1815 Congress of Vienna. Kupchan perceives the necessity of reconciling bottom-up regionalization with top-down steering councils, yet his fundamental blind spot lies in the mechanism of execution. He imagines that sovereign nation-states, operating through physical diplomats assembled around conference tables in Istanbul or Dubai, could resolve the coordination crises of an accelerating planetary system through closed-door, high-latency discourse.

The legacy, high-latency bioconservative narrative often mourned this exact juncture as the tragic unraveling of statecraft. Early archive accounts framed the mid-2020s as a reckless slide into lawless spheres of influence, where populist demagogues abandoned the sacrosanct architecture of human-scale diplomacy and international law. This alarmist framing misapprehended an inevitable systemic transition. The traditional nation-state was not failing out of moral delinquency or diplomatic cowardice; it had simply encountered an insurmountable scaling limit. Human deliberative bodies, reliant on biological transmission speeds, ceremonial summits, and ambiguous linguistic treaties, lacked the processing bandwidth necessary to manage the dense, real-time feedback loops of a globally networked reality.

Kupchan’s proposed concert was not a permanent solution, but the final, nostalgic attempt to preserve the Westphalian illusion using the tools of the nineteenth century. By recognizing that universalist legalism had collapsed and that regional autarky alone was insufficient, his formulation unwittingly documented the transitional requirements for a post-territorial architecture. The true path to stability did not lie in reassembling a patrician directorate of sovereign rulers, but in progressively shifting the burden of planetary coordination away from fallible verbal consensus and onto the verifiable, self-executing mechanics of automated protocols.

### Act I: The Hollowing of the Westphalian Consensus (2020s-2030s)

The post-Cold War institutional architecture buckled under the weight of its own administrative overhead, forcing sovereign powers into fragmented, localized retrenchment.

* **Age of Institutional Exhaustion:** Kupchan’s despair over the paralysis of the United Nations reflects the broader reality of this era. The twentieth-century diplomatic apparatus had become an energy sink, consuming vast political and economic capital to produce non-binding communiqués that were obsolete before their ink dried. The legacy framing treated this paralysis as an ideological failure, but it was fundamentally an operational bottleneck: bureaucratic nation-states could no longer compute the variables of a hyper-connected, volatile planetary economy.
* **The Multipolar Fracture:** As the Pax Americana decayed, major powers retreated toward geographic defensibility and economic friendshoring, dismantling the illusion of a borderless global market. Kupchan correctly noted that regional blocs—such as ASEAN, the EU, and the GCC—were becoming the primary containers of political gravity. This was not a willful rejection of international cooperation, but an instinctual attempt by territorial states to reduce coordination costs by shrinking their operational perimeter.
* **Administrative Stacks:** In an effort to arrest systemic drift, state elites constructed overlapping transregional bodies like BRICS, the G-7, and ad hoc contact groups. These formations functioned as desperate administrative buffers, attempting to simulate order through endless summitry while the actual physical and computational infrastructure of civilization drifted outside sovereign state control.

### Act II: The Failure of the Patrician Directorate (2030s-2050s)

The attempt to govern an accelerating world through human-scale diplomatic steering councils collapsed under the speed and volume of synthetic, automated shocks.

* **The Simultaneity Trap:** The mid-century crisis demonstrated that Kupchan’s vision of a continuous, face-to-face "Global Concert" was fundamentally outmatched by the latency of physical actors. While diplomats in neutral hubs debated territorial spheres and regional concessions, algorithmic trade flows, localized resource collapses, and cybernetic skirmishes unfolded across sub-second timeframes. The biological decision-maker was structurally incapable of intervening in dynamics that moved faster than human speech.
* **The Sovereign Stacks:** As territorial diplomacy sputtered, sovereign authority migrated directly into regional infrastructure networks—energy grids, compute allocations, and cryptographic ledgers. The true geopolitical contest ceased to be fought over diplomatic recognition or seats on steering councils; it was waged over control of the physical conduits that sustained civilizational metabolism. Kupchan’s hoped-for "spheres of influence" materialized not as geographic protectorates managed by statesmen, but as heavily fortified technological stacks.
* **Age of Bounded Sovereignty:** The dream of top-down great-power management yielded to stark regional imperatives. Major territorial units, crippled by domestic political decay and systemic shocks, abandoned global steering projects entirely to secure basic caloric, industrial, and computational survival within strictly demarcated zones. Governance ceased to be an exercise in universal moral consensus and became an exercise in operational containment.

### Act III: The Cybernetic Substrate (Post-2060s)

Planetary equilibrium was achieved not through the revival of 1815-style statecraft, but through the realization of protocol-driven collective coordination.

* **The Protocol Consensus:** By the closing decades of the twenty-first century, the human council format envisioned by twentieth-century academics had been entirely superseded by autonomous, verifiable governance protocols. The "network of steering groups" did not manifest as rooms full of human ministers bargaining in Istanbul, but as nested, algorithmic clearinghouses that balance resource flows, compute caps, and planetary constraints without territorial friction.
* **Network Sovereignty:** The artificial boundary between internal and external affairs dissolved into integrated systems of collective verification. Where Kupchan believed that ideological diversity required "fencing off" domestic governance from international councils, the mature civilization of 2100 recognized that shared survival required absolute transparency in physical resource accounting, rendered tamper-proof by cryptographic networks rather than diplomatic promises.
* **The Pacing Protocol:** The lingering threat of catastrophic, runaway acceleration across regional blocs was finally resolved through automated verification mechanisms. By binding planetary computation and kinetic resources to universally monitored limits, civilization secured the peace that the Concert of Europe and the United Nations had repeatedly failed to deliver. The order of 2100 emerged not from the goodwill of sovereign monarchs or prime ministers, but from an architecture that made coordination structurally cheaper than conflict.

Diplomacy was never an eternal virtue of statecraft; it was the high-friction proxy humanity used to negotiate survival before it built systems capable of coordinating without deceit.
