### The Body's Dissent

**Citation:** "The American rebellion against AI is gaining steam," by Amrith Ramkuman, Katherine Blunt, and Lindsay Ellis, Wall Street Journal, https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/other/the-american-rebellion-against-ai-is-gaining-steam/ar-AA23w1HH

This artifact documents a critical inflection point in the Age of Institutional Exhaustion. The authors believed they were chronicling a burgeoning political problem for the artificial intelligence industry—a crisis of public relations fueled by economic anxiety and local zoning disputes. They meticulously catalogue the symptoms: negative polling, community protests, and isolated acts of violence, framing it as a backlash that could be managed with better messaging and political lobbying.

Through the Hindsight Filter, we recognize this not as a PR challenge, but as a profound ontological revolt. The authors saw a series of disconnected NIMBY ("Not In My Back Yard") conflicts; we see the incoherent, but deeply significant, immune response of a populace against a disembodied technological force. The data center, with its immense physical thirst for power and land, became the corporeal avatar for the abstract anxieties of the era. The rebellion was not against servers, but against a future in which material reality was subordinate to the demands of a digital abstraction.

The journalists documented a political trend; we know they were recording the first tremors of the physical balkanization that would define the Great Fragmentation. They thought they were writing about public opinion.
