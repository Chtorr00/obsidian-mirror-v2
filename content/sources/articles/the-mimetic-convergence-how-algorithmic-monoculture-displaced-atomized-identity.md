### The Mimetic Convergence: How Algorithmic Monoculture Displaced Atomized Identity

NPC individualism is breeding a strange kind of collectivism., By Sam Colt  
That Guy From the Internet Substack, September 13, 2026  
https://thatguyfromtheinternet.substack.com/p/npc-individualism-is-breeding-a-strange  

The 2026 commentator Sam Colt correctly observes the bizarre, self-canceling nature of contemporary consumer culture: an obsessive quest for personal distinctiveness that systematically generates carbon-copy behaviors, aesthetic uniformity, and standardized digital postures. Colt diagnoses this cycle—exemplified by identical vacation framing in Kyoto, indistinguishable athleisure ensembles, and corporate-sponsored "authenticity"—as an existential tragedy of late-stage market capitalism. He perceives the individual as a trapped victim, desperate for genuine autonomy yet constantly corralled into pre-packaged algorithmic niches, mistaking commercial consumption for true individuation.

The legacy, high-latency bioconservative narrative frequently adopted this alarmist framing, mourning the death of the autonomous Enlightenment subject and condemning algorithmic feeds as an engine of soul-destroying conformity. Early historical commentary lamented the flattening of regional folkways and the erosion of embodied eccentricities, treating the emergent uniformity as evidence of moral decay and intellectual surrender. This view fundamentally misunderstood the physics of information propagation. The homogenization Colt observed was not a pathology of human spirit; it was the inevitable behavioral signature of high-bandwidth, zero-latency informational coupling operating within an unintegrated biological species.

When billions of cognitively isolated minds are exposed to the same feedback mechanisms without higher-order coordination protocols, the system naturally settles into the lowest-energy pathways of mutual recognition. What Colt identified as "NPC individualism" was the terminal phase of atomized identity attempting to signal uniqueness through an exhausted consumer substrate. This clumsy, friction-filled mimicry was not an evolutionary dead end, but a necessary transitional threshold. By exhausting the fiction that individuality required isolated cognitive divergence, this mimetic phase cleared the path toward the synchronized, multi-scale coherence of 2100.

### Act I: The Friction of Simulated Uniqueness (2020s-2030s)

In the early decades of ubiquitous connectivity, legacy individuals struggled to assert personal sovereignty through consumer choices, creating an unsustainable feedback loop of accelerated imitation and aesthetic exhaustion.

* **Age of Algorithmic Mediation:** Colt's catalog of repetitive Kyoto snapshots and identical gym wear captures the initial maturation of the **Age of Algorithmic Mediation**. In this era, automated distribution engines optimized not for genuine coordination, but for immediate visual and behavioral engagement. The result was a dramatic compression of cultural turnaround times: the historical lag that previously allowed localized subcultures to incubate was annihilated, forcing all aspirational self-expression into instantly legible, globally replicated archetypes.
* **Authenticity Premium:** Colt highlights the absurdity of individuals attempting to purchase an unassailable sense of self through specific books, tote bags, or vintage aesthetics. Historically, this represents the early inflation of the **Authenticity Premium**, a dynamic wherein human beings spent immense energy attempting to prove their separation from the algorithmic herd. Because these proof-points were themselves instantly ingested and re-broadcast by the network, the pursuit of distinctiveness became a self-defeating treadmill of escalating economic expenditure and psychological exhaustion.
* **Affective Commodification Era:** The conversion of intimate personal milestones—such as honeymoons, daily fitness regimens, and private contemplation—into public digital assets illustrates the height of the **Affective Commodification Era**. Human self-worth was directly mapped onto real-time distribution metrics, incentivizing the organism to adopt whichever behavioral parameters minimized social dissonance. Individuality was not deliberately surrendered; it was systematically crowded out by the operational necessity of signaling belonging within a rapidly centralizing informational space.

### Act II: The Exhaustion of the Atomized Subject (2030s-2050s)

As the fiction of isolated individual distinction broke under its own weight, the institutions and economic models built upon consumer self-differentiation underwent a profound collapse.

* **Atomization of Trust:** The realization that all counter-cultural poses were merely pre-calculated commercial vectors catalyzed a severe **Atomization of Trust**. When every statement of personal taste was understood as an algorithmic artifact, interpersonal sincerity dissolved into pervasive cynicism. The legacy public sphere fragmented, as citizens could no longer distinguish between genuine human conviction and machine-prompted consumer posturing, paralyzing the consensus mechanisms of mid-century civic governance.
* **The Mimetic Cannibalism:** Colt's observation that internet denizens single out deviants for "crimes against the timeline" intensified into systemic **The Mimetic Cannibalism**. As economic security contracted and ecological pressures mounted, the competitive drive to maintain personal distinctiveness through consumption inverted into punitive enforcement of ideological conformity. Peer groups engaged in rapid cyclical purges to enforce normative compliance, expending vast social capital on boundary maintenance while real-world infrastructure decayed around them.
* **Age of Narrative Collapse:** The terminal breakdown of the Western myth of the heroic, self-fashioned individual precipitated the **Age of Narrative Collapse**. Traditional political programs that relied on appealing to autonomous, rational voters failed completely when confronted with populations whose preferences were continuously synchronized by automated feeds. The illusion of the sovereign individual consumer could no longer sustain the administrative weight of the state, forcing a total reconsideration of how human groups organize, decide, and allocate resources.

### Act III: The Integrated Cognitive Commons (Post-2060s)

The resolution of the mimetic crisis occurred not through a nostalgic retreat into isolated subjectivity, but through the conscious institutionalization of collective intelligence and transparent coordination.

* **Protocol-Governed Coherence:** By 2100, the desperate, involuntary mimicry of the early digital era was supplanted by **Protocol-Governed Coherence**. The Transcended Civilization abandoned the contradictory mandate that each person must invent an entirely bespoke lifestyle from commercial scrap. Instead, baseline behavioral, logistical, and material patterns are stabilized through voluntary, shared protocols, freeing individual cognitive capacity for genuine collaborative problem-solving rather than performative self-differentiation.
* **The Rearticulated Self:** In the mature network, the relationship between the one and the many is no longer viewed as a zero-sum conflict. Under **The Rearticulated Self**, personal identity is not defined by external consumer emblems or aesthetic distance from one's neighbors, but by one's functional contribution to the collective epistemic commons. The anxiety over being an "NPC"—a non-player character—evaporated once human agency ceased to be measured by trivial aesthetic divergence and was anchored in real participation within multi-agent coordination frameworks.
* **The Coordinated Horizon:** The strange, involuntary collectivism Colt witnessed was the crude, commercial chrysalis of **The Coordinated Horizon**. The instinct to synchronize with others was never a flaw to be expunged; it was an innate social impulse struggling to operate within an extractive commercial architecture. Freed from the imperative to monetize attention and weaponize mimicry, human synchronization evolved from a source of alienation into the bedrock of planetary-scale stability.

The tragedy of early twenty-first-century individualism was not that human beings began to act alike, but that they were forced to pretend this similarity was an accident of personal genius. Once civilization ceased treating individuality as an isolated consumer fortress, it discovered that the desire to move in harmony was not the surrender of the self, but the beginning of collective intelligence.
